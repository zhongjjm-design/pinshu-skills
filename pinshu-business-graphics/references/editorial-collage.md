# Editorial Collage

## Identity

Build an argument from evidence fragments: photography, documents, annotations, numbers, captions, and deliberate cropping. It should feel edited, not decorated.

Use for:

- report openers, industry observation, trend narratives
- mixed qualitative evidence and visual context
- human, market, cultural, or operational stories needing texture

Avoid when exact comparison, auditability, or a clean system diagram is primary.

## Composition families

1. `evidence wall`: one dominant image plus two or three annotated evidence fragments.
2. `document splice`: report excerpts, highlighted lines, stamps, and one interpretive headline.
3. `before-after archive`: two temporal states joined by dates, captions, and a decisive transition.
4. `field-note spread`: contact-sheet rhythm, marginal notes, and a numbered observation sequence.

## Image and sourcing rules

- Give every fragment a role: context, proof, contrast, or consequence.
- Prefer user-supplied or properly sourced material.
- Do not imitate a living publication’s exact layout, masthead, or proprietary marks.
- Keep source notes for photographs and documents when required.
- Generated documentary imagery must be labeled as illustrative when it could be mistaken for evidence.

## Typography and color

- Use editorial hierarchy: headline, deck, caption, folio, source.
- Mix at most two type voices.
- Use a restrained paper, ink, or newsprint system with one signal color.
- Cropping may create tension; it must not obscure the thesis.

## Prompt kernel

> Design an editorial-collage business visual around the argument “[thesis]”. Use one dominant [image role], two or three sourced evidence fragments, concise Chinese captions, a numbered reading path, and a restrained [paper/ink] palette. Make each fragment serve context, proof, contrast, or consequence. No mood-board randomness, no fake logos, no invented documents, no scrapbook stickers, no generic stock-business people.

## Failure boundaries

Reject when:

- the page is a mood board with no argument
- fragments lack captions or evidence roles
- decoration is mistaken for documentary proof
- more than five competing fragments destroy hierarchy
- the layout copies a known publication too literally
