# Writing: steps 1, 3, 4 and 7, and the thinking-through that closes step 2

The writer is the session that talks with the author, one agent from asking him to the last edit. It carries everything the author said, so nothing is lost in a handoff. It checks its own work two ways: it reads the draft through and rewrites by the paragraph (step 4), and it checks what can be counted (the script); every judgment about whether the piece holds, is right and keeps the reader comes from the four positions (`5-review.md`) and the author.

The order is the order a writer works in: ask the author what he thinks and has lived through (step 1); find the matter out in full (step 2); then, with the material in hand, think it through and answer the three questions (gate 1); then build the piece (step 3). The three questions decide how the article is built and told; they do not decide what research looks for.

## Step 1: ask the author

What the author thinks and has lived through comes only from asking him, so the writer asks before anything is researched or answered for him. This is a conversation, not a gate.

1. When the seed is someone else's article or title, write one sentence first: why this caught the author, and what it promises the reader. When the author picked it for its title, the question that title asks is the question this article answers; one company or case in the source does not make the article about that company. When the seed or the author's words place the piece in a series, write one more sentence: what the earlier pieces covered and what this one adds.
2. **Count the author's own material in the seed**: things he did, saw or was told, numbers he has, his judgment. Few of them means the asking matters more; the piece is not written on material the model makes up.
3. **First judge from the seed what the piece hangs on** (item 8 asks for it, so it comes before the asking): a view or the author's own experience, or one event, report, company or tool. Then **ask, one question at a time**, in this order, skipping what the seed already makes plain:
   1. Read the point back in one sentence: "您想说的是……，对吗？" He corrects it.
   2. The vaguest word in it: "您说的××，具体指什么？举一个您碰到过的例子。"
   3. Who it is for, what they work in, the moment they get stuck. What he does not know is written "待查" and goes to research.
   4. What happened to him: what he did, saw, heard, the numbers, the words someone said. When the piece hangs on an event, report, company or tool (a piece after the news, a case analysis, a report reading), do not ask for his experience; ask "您怎么看这件事？哪句您认？" A piece that hangs on his view or his experience is asked as above.
   5. Where the reasoning jumps: "从这里到那个结论，中间缺了什么？" Then both ways: "为什么" for what holds it up, "所以呢" for what the reader does with it.
4. **His words first.** Write down what he says before any reading of it; the writer's own reading is marked "我的理解" and never written as his words. When the writer would join two things he said, it asks "这两件您看连得上吗" instead of announcing the link. What he cannot answer is written "还不知道" and the asking moves on.
5. **When to stop**: the writer can write, in his words, the point, the vague word made concrete, the reader and the moment they get stuck, and the missing step; or he says "可以了". Usually five or six questions.
6. **Settle the line here.** When his words hold several separate lines, ask which one this article follows; the others stay in the point map marked "另一篇".
7. **Name the expert's field**: one line, "专家身份：××行当的从业者", the field of practice this topic belongs to (`5-review.md`, the expert).
8. Write into the decision log: the point in one sentence, his experiences in his words, what is 待查, the line chosen, the expert's field, and what the piece hangs on (judged at the start of item 3: a view or his own experience; or one event, company, report or tool), which sets what research looks for (`2-research.md`).

When the author is not there to answer, the route waits here; the writer never answers for him. A later piece in a series whose point was asked before, or a piece the author says to take straight on, goes to step 2 with the seed as it is, and the decision log says so.

Done when the author's point, his own material and the gaps are written down in his words, and he has said the read-back is right.

## The end of step 2: think it through, then the three questions (gate 1)

Research has found the matter out in full (`2-research.md`). The writer reads the whole material pack and the author's points, then:

1. **One page of thinking-through**, three to five hundred characters, in plain paragraphs, not a table: the question the article answers and the answer (the author's words in quotation marks); why it holds, step by step, with his own experience and inference marked apart; the strongest objection in the material and how the article answers it or narrows its claim; the reader: who, what they work in, the moment they get stuck, what they can do tomorrow; where it is still shallow, read against the standard's 第十七节 items 2 and 3, two or three lines in the writer's own view, marked "AI 补" (the writer's own view; at gate 1 the author judges whether it is thought through). From the material, bring in what goes further than the author's view (someone who sees it deeper, a counter-example, a newer practice), each marked as brought by AI, for the author to take or leave.
2. Write the topic in one sentence: the question this article answers. Then answer the good-article standard's 动笔前先答三问 (第十六节) from the page and run the checks it gives there. Question 1 is answered from the author's words and the profile's reader; the reader's tools and stuck moment may come from research, but a case found in research is never turned backwards into the reason the reader opens it.
3. **One question, one answer.** The third answer answers the topic's question and only that one: write "选题问的是（做法／原因／结果／谁），我答的是（　）". The two kinds differ, or the answer lays two things side by side (each holds on its own, the one still reads with the other taken away, neither grows out of the other): rewrite it, or split it into two articles. One layer pushing on to the next is one judgment, whatever word joins it: the second half grows out of the first (what, why, what to do; "you think A, and beneath A there is B"). Tell the two apart by that, not by connecting words. Every key word in the answers says who did what; a word the author would have to ask about is replaced by plain words.
4. Check the seed's range words first: `python3 scripts/audit_draft.py <the page and the three answers> --card --seed <the seed saved verbatim as a file>`. It flags range words the author's own words do not have; a flag is not an error, it goes with its sentence into the gate 1 message as a question (`stops.md`). Then write the page and the three answers into the decision log and show them at gate 1 (`stops.md`). When the author says a part is shallow, ask him about that part only, or research that part only, and rewrite the page.

Done when each answer is a sentence someone could say "不对" to, and still matters to the reader with the company and case names removed, the third answer holds one judgment that answers the topic's question, and the author has read the page and said it is thought through.

## Step 3: title, outline, first section (gate 2)

From the three answers, the thinking-through page and the material pack (step 2):

1. **Type**: the standard's seven types (第十六节); it sets whether the author appears and how the piece opens and ends.
2. **Exemplars.** Find two or three whole articles by mature writers, in this order: the author's approved list (configuration `exemplar_index`); the configuration's `exemplar_fallback`; the library's writer collections (configuration `material_folders`); then online, the leading business and brand media the profile names as benchmarks. Never the account's own old articles: each is still a trial that has not broken out, and a trial taken as the standard keeps the account where it is. One that clearly breaks out (`6-deliver.md`, "After publishing", every 8 articles) is taken apart and may become one. An exemplar holds both: it reads well and readers or the trade recognise it, and its numbers are good (reads, shares). Pick by how they read, not by type alone: a person telling what happened to them and what they make of it, sentence handing to sentence. A method or checklist piece taken as the exemplar pulls the draft toward a manual. For each, one line: what to learn from it (how it opens, how one part hands on to the next, how a case comes in and what follows it). The writer writes the opening and first section after its recommended one or two; at gate 2 the author picks, and when he picks others, the opening and first section are rewritten after his picks before anything else. The picked ones are added to `exemplar_index`. Without an exemplar a model falls back on its average shape (three neat parts, a verdict closing every section, report-speak), which is what reads as machine-written.
3. **The hook**: the one point most likely to make the reader open it, taken from the three answers (第十六节, item 2), written with one of the standard's eight title methods (第六节).
4. **Title and opening candidates from two vendors.** The writer drafts its own candidates, and at the same time a model of another vendor (configuration `hook_second`) drafts six title-and-opening pairs with prompt "Title and opening candidates" (`prompts.md`); different vendors give different hooks. Before showing any, every candidate passes two checks: an ordinary reader understands it at a glance (no insider abbreviations such as a brand's initials, no wording that reads two ways); with the company and case names removed, the reader still cares. A candidate failing either is dropped, never shown. Every number in a candidate is checked against the material; two shares divided into a multiple, or "calls" turned into "output", is an invented fact. From all that pass, the writer offers three titles at gate 2, each with its method and source vendor.
5. **How the piece moves**: one plain paragraph on how the article goes from its first paragraph to its last: what the reader wonders at the start, where in the whole piece the single most valuable point lands (as a share of the length; the standard's 第二节 item 3 says where readers leave), what each part answers, how each part grows out of the one before and hands the reader to the next, where the reader learns something new, what they leave with. This is the author's 起承转合: the writer knows the logic before writing, and every paragraph is joined to its neighbours. Name the thread that runs through the whole piece: the author's own experience when he has one (his words, the decision log, the library's records), with other people's experience woven into it as support. **How to cite**, by the kind of material (research marks each item's kind): a classic view of someone with real weight (德鲁克, 科特勒, a line from 刘润's books, a recognised expert) is named, with the source or the book; common sense and long-standing management or economic principles belong to no one and carry no name (not everything in 刘润's articles is his own view); someone's concrete practice (how 黄叔, 归藏 or 宝玉 does it) is named, it is what they really did; a line from a random person online (X, a search result) is never quoted as a view. A piece that goes "A 说、B 说、C 说", or hands each section to a different stranger, reads as pieced together.
6. **Outline**: one spine (the question every section answers); each section as the one judgment its subhead will say (第八节), with the material it stands on named under it; the place where the article takes a stand someone could disagree with (第五节). A section stands on what an opinion piece really has: the author's experience, his reasoning, an everyday scene readers have lived, a hypothetical example that shows the point ("假设一家公司……", "比如你是……"; a tool of argument, not a claim of fact, the standard's 第五节), or a case. **When a section stands on a case, the case comes from the material pack or the author's own experience and passes two checks**: the people and actions in it match the section's judgment (who it is about, what they actually did, what came of it, each found in the case; when they do not match, take another case, and when simplifying a case keep any order, limit or feedback that changes the conclusion); and the reader meets this kind of thing in his own work. A case that fails either is mentioned in a line at most. In an opinion or self-dissection piece, a section never stands on an outside study the reader never meets in his work; in a report-reading piece the report is the subject. A piece that does not use the author's experience (step 1, item 3) has at least one section standing on a specific person or scene: pick a real person and event from the material pack, and when there is none use a hypothetical example, marked as one (the standard's 第五节); gate 2 says which section it is. A section with nothing to stand on is said plainly at gate 2: the author is asked, or the section is cut. This is where material is chosen; research only found it. Then one line for the author: the title's question, then the subheads in order joined by commas into one sentence. It goes into the gate 2 message for the author to judge whether it turns; the writer sets no pass line on it.
7. **Opening and first section**: written out in full, to the standard's 第五、七、九节, the type's card and what the picked exemplars show, as step 4 says. The author reads real sentences here, not only an outline: how it reads is judged before the rest is written.

Show them at gate 2 (`stops.md`). Decisions for the author come only from the three kinds `stops.md` names. Done when the author has approved the title, how the piece moves, the outline, the opening and first section, and picked the exemplars, in his own words in the decision log.

## Step 4: writing

1. **Read before writing.** The standard's 利他 paragraph first (第一节, 利他是根): every paragraph is written for what the reader takes from it. Then the picked exemplars, whole: how they open, how one part leads to the next, how a case comes in and what follows it, the voice. Learn the movement and the voice; take none of their facts or sentences. Then the standard's 第五节 (活人感: sentences that breathe, plain words, a person judging) and 第九节 (paragraphs and how they join). Read them again before every rewrite.
2. **Write the way the standard's 第五、九节 say**, paragraphs, sentences and joins alike. Write from the seed (whole), the three answers, the approved title, how the piece moves, the outline, the approved opening and first section, and the material pack; length comes from the selected account's configuration.
3. After the opening and the first section, do the check the standard gives (第十六节, 写完开头和第一节): is the core still the outline's? Changed: stop and go back to gate 2. Unchanged: write to the end without stopping.
4. **Read it through, then fix by the paragraph.** Read the whole draft from the top as the reader would. Mark every sentence you had to read twice, every "它", "这", "那" whose thing is not in the sentence before, every paragraph that says two things, every place two sections join badly. Rewrite those paragraphs whole; patches make it worse.
5. Run `audit_draft.py --first-draft` with the configuration's length and banned-word file. Fix blockers. Its "reads heavy" and "reads flat" warnings (sentences packed with commas or 的, runs of same-length sentences) point to places to reread, not to rules: fix the ones that read badly. Check the countable items of the standard's 第十八节 (title length, subheads read in a row, paragraph length, sources named). A fallback writer's draft goes through items 4 and 5 too.

Done when the draft is complete, has been read through and fixed by the paragraph, the script shows no blocker, and the draft goes to step 5.

## Step 7: revision

The writer revises once, from everything together (`5-review.md`, "Merging"): the editor's first-pass list, the reader's spots, the expert's report, the fact checker's must-fixes. Each comes in its own shape (the editor gives items, the reader its words, the expert judgments, the fact checker table lines) and is handled in that shape:

0. **First list what stays word for word**: the title and opening the author approved at gate 2, and any sentence he edited by hand or asked to keep. Changing one of them is asked of the author first.
1. Fact and copying items: every one fixed (deleted, or replaced with something the source supports). 待补: add the source or delete the unfound point, never a soft word.
2. Reader items: each "here I would stop" gets a change at that place, or a reason the author sees at gate 3.
3. Editor items: adopted when the reader would rather read the result; each adopted cut or narrowing carries one line, "读者更想读了，还是只是更严谨了"; a fix never removes a specific number, person or practice, except a name the citation rule says not to carry (step 3, item 5).
4. Expert items: 说满了 covers facts and inferences said too fully (one case made a general rule, "may" turned into "will"), and gets its condition added (who, when, how far) in a sentence of its own, the judgment keeping its edge. A line where the author states his view hard ("能花钱买到的，都不算数") gets no condition: when the reasoning before it does not hold it up, the reasoning is what gets fixed, never a "当然" after the line; every other sourced finding gets a change or a reason. A 站不住 is the author's (`5-review.md`, "Grading") and does not come here.
5. Rewrite whole sections where the list points; keep the voice; never bring back what the author cut, never drop a source. A condition, a qualifier or a source the review asks for goes into a sentence of its own, or replaces words, never into the sentence it qualifies, so the judgment keeps its edge. Length is held by the configuration's maximum, not sentence by sentence; how long a sentence runs follows the standard's 第五节 (sentences that breathe: few long ones, none the reader cannot get through in one breath, and not every one cut short). When the author or a reviewer says the piece does not read, reread the exemplars and the standard's 第五、九节 first, then rewrite by the paragraph.
6. Lay the new title, opening and three sharpest lines beside the previous version's. If the new version went flatter (a stand turned into advice, a sting removed, a hook gone), redo that part before anything else.
7. Read the changed paragraphs aloud in your head with the paragraph before and after (step 4, item 4), then run `audit_draft.py` without `--first-draft`; the hard maximum now blocks.
8. **The change list**, one line per change: 改前（原句）→ 改后（新句）→ 为什么改 (which deliverable, its item, the reader's words or the table line copied as they are); an editor item adds 对应第几条意见. A change that touches meaning adds one more clause to 为什么改: whether the original meaning is kept (who did it, whether it happened, cause, range, degree; "愿意做" turned into "做过" or "有时" into "一定" has changed it); a sentence the writer marks "意思变了" says what changed. Every item not changed is listed too: "未改，理由：……". The same list is gate 3's "what the revision changed".
9. **Hand in four things together** for the editor's second pass: the editor's first-pass list verbatim, the revised draft, the change list, the reader's spots verbatim. Missing one, nothing has been handed in.

The standard's 第十七节 sets the size: sentences and words are a revision; moving paragraphs or subheads means rereading the whole; changing the core judgment, the main case or the skeleton is a rewrite, which goes to the author: the organizer recommends gate 1 when the judgment itself is wrong and gate 2 when the draft wandered off a sound judgment, and the author decides where it goes back.

A second-pass 退回写手 brings the writer back here once more, with the same change list and the same four things. Done when every fact item is closed, every reader and expert item is changed or has a reason, the script passes, and the four things are handed in.

## When the session cannot write

When the session cannot write a long Chinese article (out of quota, or a model that cannot), the configuration's `writer_fallback` writes. This is the last resort: a handoff loses what was said (the author: "一转述一交工单就会变形，或者就会丢掉一些东西"), so only originals go over, never a summary. Before writing, the fallback writer says in one sentence what it understands the author wants to say, and writes only after the author nods. Its brief holds only these, in this order:

1. Who reads it and the sentence on what the seed promises them.
2. The seed, whole and verbatim; the author's answers in step 1 and his words at each gate, verbatim; the thinking-through page.
3. The approved title, how the piece moves, the outline, and the opening and first section the author approved, verbatim ("从这里往下写").
4. The one or two exemplars the author picked at gate 2, whole, each introduced in one sentence on what to learn ("学它的口气和推进，不借它的事实和句子").
5. The material pack: facts and cases with sources, each marked "可以用"; "需确认" items say so, with the wording to use.
6. Whom it is written for and how it should read: the standard's 第一节 from 利他是根 to the paragraph before 好稿是, then 第五节 and 第九节, pasted whole (`<paste:>`, `prompts.md`, "Every brief", item 1). Then three bottom lines, pasted as written:

```text
1. 人名、公司、数字、引语、日期，以及作者本人和别人做过的事、结果，只用上面给的，不改事情本身，不编没发生的事；情绪、场景、口气可以写，合情合理就行。
2. 文章里不透露用 AI 写稿：不写写稿流程、写稿工具、用 AI 写稿这件事。
3. 全文 <length>。
```

7. Output: only the article, `#` title, `##` subheads, no "---" dividers in the body, then 参考文章 as the standard's 第十三节 says.

Apart from the standard's 利他 paragraph (第一节) and its 第五、九节, which say whom the writing serves and how it should read, the standard and the edit rules stay with the editor and the checks after writing; the brief carries what to write, not a rule book. Its revision uses `prompts.md`, "Fallback writer: revise". Record "writer: <model>, fallback" in the editor notes.

## After 定稿: learn from the author's edits

When the author has edited the draft by hand, save the writer's last draft in `原稿库` and the author's version in `终稿库` of `edit_pairs_folder`, both named by date and title. Compare them sentence by sentence and write what the author did into `修改规律.md` as observations first, each with its "原稿 → 终稿" example and the article it came from: one edit may only be that article's wording, punctuation or sentence. A move made the same way across several articles becomes a rule, marked with how many articles it rests on. The edit rules reach the editor position (`5-review.md`) only when they rest on two or more articles, and it checks the next draft's voice against them; a one-article observation waits. Entries written before 3.9 carry no count yet: until they are counted, those still in force reach the editor as before, and the editor notes say so. The author's voice is learned from his edits, never from the account's old articles.

## Time

Measured, not chased: record the start and end of each step in the decision log. What saves time is a right start (gates 1 and 2) and one revision, not fewer checks.
